# Abstract

The dissertation evaluates the ability of deep sequence models to predict fixed-delta implied volatility (IV) surfaces for S&P 500 index options and evaluates their economic value through implementable trading strategies. The analysis uses daily SPX option data from 1996 to 2022, organized into a 10-maturity × 18-delta grid structure with light no-arbitrage regularisation and rolling context features. The research investigates two separate models: an LSTM network with an attention mechanism for historical window weighting, and a ConvLSTM model that applies spatial-temporal convolutions to the delta-tenor grid. The training process includes walk-forward splits with no-arbitrage-aware penalties, horizon weighting, and early stopping.

Forecast accuracy is evaluated using multiple metrics, including MAE, RMSE, QLIKE, vega-weighted errors, and structural diagnostics. The attention maps show which time periods contain the most important information, while the ConvLSTM model maintains a consistent relationship between different maturity dates. The ConvLSTM model demonstrates better structural fidelity than the other models while achieving higher accuracy than econometric benchmarks.

The economic value of the models is evaluated through a 30-day short strangle strategy with dynamic delta-hedging, VIX-based entry gates and stop-losses, and explicit cost accounting. Across common-window backtests from 2004 to 2022, the ConvLSTM strategy delivers the strongest results, with an annualised return of 31.3% and a Sharpe ratio of 1.37, compared with 24.0% and 0.96 for the rule-based benchmark. ConvLSTM also shows greater resilience in high-VIX periods. These findings highlight how attention-based and spatio-temporal deep learning models can capture valuable structure in the IV surface, linking forecast accuracy to robust, risk-controlled trading performance.
